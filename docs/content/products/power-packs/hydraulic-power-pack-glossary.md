# 3. Comprehensive Glossary (A-Z)

### Accumulator
A pressure vessel integrated into a hydraulic circuit that stores potential energy in the form of compressed gas (usually nitrogen) pushing against hydraulic fluid. Used in power packs to deliver sudden bursts of high-volume flow, absorb pressure spikes, or maintain pressure while the pump is turned off.

### Cavitation
A destructive phenomenon where localized low pressure causes fluid to vaporize, creating microscopic bubbles. When these bubbles pass into high-pressure zones inside the pump, they collapse violently, ripping metal away from the pump internals and destroying the power pack.

### CETOP Standard
Comité Européen des Transmissions Oléohydrauliques et Pneumatiques. A European standard defining the mounting interface (port patterns) for hydraulic directional control valves (e.g., CETOP 3 / NG6, CETOP 5 / NG10). This ensures valves from different manufacturers can be mounted on the same manifold block.

### Directional Control Valve (DCV)
A valve mounted on the power pack's manifold that dictates the path of the pressurized fluid. It determines whether a hydraulic cylinder extends, retracts, or holds its position.

### High-Low Pump Circuit
A dual-pump configuration commonly used in press power packs. It utilizes a high-volume/low-pressure pump for rapid cylinder approach, and a low-volume/high-pressure pump for the actual pressing operation. This saves massive amounts of electrical energy.

### Manifold Block
A solid block of CNC-machined metal (usually steel or aluminum) containing internal drilled galleries that route hydraulic fluid between different valves. It eliminates messy, leak-prone external piping on a power pack.

### Prime Mover
The initial source of mechanical energy in a power pack. In industrial manufacturing, this is almost always a 3-phase or single-phase electric motor. In mobile applications, it can be a DC motor or an internal combustion engine.

### Proportional Valve
Unlike standard on/off (bang-bang) valves, proportional valves can partially open, allowing a PLC to infinitely control the exact flow rate and pressure. Used in advanced power packs to smoothly control cylinder acceleration and deceleration.

### Regenerative Circuit
A specialized hydraulic circuit where fluid exhausted from the rod end of a cylinder is routed directly back into the cap end (instead of returning to the tank). This vastly increases the extension speed of the cylinder without requiring a larger hydraulic pump.

### Relief Valve
The primary safety mechanism in any hydraulic power pack. It acts as a fuse, automatically opening and dumping fluid back into the reservoir if the system pressure exceeds a safely calibrated limit.

### Reservoir (Tank)
The steel container at the base of a power pack. Beyond just holding fluid, it functions as the primary heat sink to cool the oil, and provides dwell time for entrained air bubbles to rise to the surface before the oil is drawn back into the pump.

### Unloading Valve
A specialized pressure-control valve used in High-Low circuits. Once the system reaches a specific pressure threshold, the unloading valve dumps the high-volume pump's flow back to the tank at zero pressure, reducing motor load and heat generation.
